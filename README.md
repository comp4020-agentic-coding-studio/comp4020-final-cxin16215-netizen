# The garden

A garden that only grows while people are in it together. Anyone can sow a
flower, and it is still there, ringed as theirs, when they come back. Nothing
grows while you are alone, and colour comes back one family at a time as more
people arrive: red with two, up to yellow with five. By day it is painted in
Monet's broken colour, by night in Van Gogh's swirling strokes, and a year
passes every eight minutes.

## What good means here

**Being together is the point.** Growth is measured in time people spend here
together, so a room full of people, like the showcase, is the garden at its most
alive. Company lingers: someone who left a few minutes ago still counts for a
while, so a stranger who arrives just after them isn't alone.

**A stranger's trace lasts.** Planting is the one thing you can do, so it has to
be worth doing: your flower keeps its place and shape, comes up every spring,
and nobody can remove it. Once all 140 places are taken, the oldest flower that
has lived a garden year withers for each new one: the garden decides, not a
person.

**It is a picture, not a dashboard.** Who is here shows first in the colour and
the growth; one line of plain words says it too, for anyone who needs it said.

**It asks for nothing.** No accounts, no notifications, no streaks. Leave and
the garden waits; come back and it has moved on without you.

**Everyone sees the same garden.** One clock from real time, the same wild
flowers, the same shape for the same flower on every screen.

## What is checked, and what is judged

Checked by `spec/` on every change: a stranger can sow on their first visit and
finds their flower, marked as theirs, when they come back; everyone else sees it,
not as theirs; only people with the garden open count as company, so nobody can
fake it with requests; nobody can remove or move a flower or learn anyone
else's identity; a log of who did what names visitors only by a short hash and
outlives a restart. Who may do what, and why, is in
[ADR 3](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-cxin16215-netizen/blob/main/docs/adr/0003-who-may-do-what.md).

Judged, not tested: whether the painting holds together in every season and
hour, checked in screenshots at both screen sizes, and whether growth feels
earned rather than slow. Whether company beats being alone was meant for the
crit, but the garden has rarely had company to judge it by.

## What I looked at

- *Gris* (2018) and *Neva* (2024) by Nomada Studio, for colour that returns as
  the world heals.
- Van Gogh's *The Starry Night* (1889) for the night, and Monet's *Poppy Field*
  (1873) and *The Magpie* (1869) for the day: unmixed dabs, violet shadow, snow
  that is never white.
- Tyler Hobbs's generative watercolour, behind every petal and hill.
- Clay Shirky's ["Situated Software"](http://shirky.com/essays/situated-software/)
  (2004): software for one group in one situation, where not scaling is a fair
  price. The garden accepts his trade: one machine, room for 140 flowers.

## What I chose not to build

Accounts and names: a visitor is whoever holds the garden's cookie. Chat, likes
or comments: the garden is the only thing people share. Removing anyone's
flower. Scores. Watching without being counted.

## Still open

The lone visitor. Since the log began on 20 October the garden has been opened
twelve times, only twice with anyone else there. A flower sown alone stays small
until two people overlap: true to the first rule, but most visitors never see
it work.

And whether the oldest flowers going is the right price for a garden that never
turns anyone away
([ADR 4](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-cxin16215-netizen/blob/main/docs/adr/0004-a-full-garden.md)):
the people who came first are the first to lose their flowers.
